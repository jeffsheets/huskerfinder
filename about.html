How It Works

Uses your device's geolocation, then searches through a list of stations that carry Husker sports. Results are sorted by the estimated signal strength at your location — or by straight-line distance to the tower if you switch the "Sort by" option — with signal bars to help you find the best reception.

Accuracy Note: We use actual FCC tower locations and licensed power data, and estimate signal strength with the same propagation curves the FCC uses to define station coverage. Real reception still depends on terrain, buildings, weather, and your radio. Try a couple of the top stations!

How Signal Strength Is Estimated

Each station's signal bars (▰▰▰▱) and the "Strongest signal" sort are calculated from the station's FCC license data and your distance from its tower:

  • FM stations are rated using the FCC's own F(50,50) propagation curves — the same curves the FCC uses to draw official coverage maps — based on the station's licensed effective radiated power (ERP) and antenna height above average terrain (HAAT). A 100,000-watt antenna on a 1,000-foot tower reaches much farther than the same power on a short one.
  • AM stations are rated with a groundwave propagation model using the station's licensed transmitter power and frequency. AM signals hug the ground, and the Great Plains has some of the best soil conductivity in the country — that's why a 50,000-watt station like KRVN 880 is listenable across most of Nebraska in the daytime, and why lower AM frequencies (590, 660, 880) carry much farther than higher ones (1400+).
  • FM translators (low-power FM repeaters of AM stations, like KLIN 99.3 FM) are capped by the FCC at 250 watts, so they're only rated strong within roughly 5–15 miles of their town — that's real, not a bug.

Why AM stations get weaker at night

At night, AM signals bounce off the ionosphere and can travel hundreds of miles ("skywave"). To keep thousands of stations from interfering with each other, the FCC licenses most AM stations with a mandatory lower nighttime power, in effect from local sunset to local sunrise. This isn't optional and can't be waived for a game broadcast — for example, KICS in Hastings runs 500 watts during the day but must drop to just 27 watts at night, while KRVN 880, KFAB 1110, and WOW 590 are engineered to keep full power around the clock (usually with directional antennas). A few stations (KTIC 840 in West Point, KUVR 1380 in Holdrege) hold daytime-only licenses and sign off completely at sunset. This app checks whether the sun is down at each station's tower and automatically uses the nighttime power — or marks daytimers "off air" — so for a 7pm kickoff, the rankings reflect what you'll actually be able to hear.

Tips for Best Results

Since radio coverage can vary greatly based on terrain, weather, tower power, and other factors:

  • Try the first few stations in your list - don't stop at just the closest one
  • Signal strength bars (▰▰▰▱) are estimates - always try the nearest towers first
  • AM stations often have better long-distance coverage, especially at night
  • FM stations typically provide clearer audio when in range
  • If you're having trouble with reception, try multiple stations from the list

Alternative Options

If you can't find a clear radio signal, you can always stream the games live at huskers.com/listen. This is especially helpful when traveling outside of Nebraska or in areas with limited radio coverage.

Technical Details

This application uses:

  • Browser geolocation API to determine your position
  • FCC (Federal Communications Commission) tower data for accurate location coordinates
  • Licensed transmitter power (AM day/night) and ERP + antenna height (FM) from FCC records
  • Distance calculations using actual tower positions
  • Interactive mapping powered by Leaflet and OpenStreetMap
  • FM signal estimates interpolated from the FCC F(50,50) propagation curve tables (47 CFR 73.333)
  • AM signal estimates from a Norton groundwave model with Great Plains soil conductivity (FCC M3 map)
  • A sunrise/sunset calculation at each tower to apply AM nighttime power restrictions

Special thanks to huskers.com for serving as a reference for station information and providing the official streaming option.

Frequently Asked Questions

What radio station plays Nebraska Husker football near me?

Use the Husker Radio Finder to find the nearest station. Click "Find Nearest Stations" to use your GPS location. Major 2026 football stations include KLIN 1400 AM / 99.3 FM (Lincoln), KRVN 880 AM (Lexington), and WOW 590 AM / KOBM 97.3 FM (Omaha). The Huskers Radio Network covers Nebraska, South Dakota, and Kansas with more than 50 stations.

What is the flagship radio station for Nebraska Huskers?

The Huskers Radio Network has several flagship stations: KLIN 1400 AM in Lincoln and KRVN 880 AM in Lexington (50,000 watts covering the Great Plains). KFAB 1110 AM in Omaha was historically a flagship station.

Can I listen to Nebraska volleyball and basketball on the radio?

Yes! Many stations broadcast Nebraska volleyball and basketball games. Use the filter checkboxes on the Radio Finder to show only volleyball (VB), men's basketball (MBB), or women's basketball (WBB) stations. The same Huskers Radio Network carries all sports.

How many radio stations broadcast Husker games?

More than 50 Husker Radio Network stations broadcasting on 60+ AM and FM frequencies (including AM/FM simulcasts) carry Nebraska Huskers games across Nebraska, South Dakota, and Kansas for the 2026-27 seasons. View the complete station list for all frequencies.

How accurate are the signal strength bars?

They're estimates built from each station's FCC license: FM uses the FCC's official F(50,50) coverage curves (power + antenna height), and AM uses a groundwave model tuned for Great Plains soil. They're good enough to rank stations against each other, which is what the "Strongest signal" sort does. Real-world reception still varies with terrain, buildings, weather, and your radio — see "How Signal Strength Is Estimated" above for the full story.

Why does an AM station I hear all day turn to static at night?

Most AM stations are required by the FCC to cut power at night (local sunset to sunrise) so their signals don't skip off the ionosphere and interfere with distant stations. Some drop dramatically — KICS Hastings goes from 500 watts to 27 watts. The finder accounts for this: after sunset it uses each station's nighttime power, so evening game rankings match reality. Big stations like KRVN 880 and WOW 590 keep full power all night.

Is my location data private?

Yes! Your location data stays entirely on your device and is never sent to any server. The distance calculations happen in your browser. We don't track or store your location.
